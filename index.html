Getting started with ThreadX

You have been handed a RealWear Navigator and told to fix a machine. This guide takes you from putting the headset on to finishing your first procedure, hands free, start to finish.

Application: ThreadX on the RealWear Navigator 500
Everything in this guide is done by voice. You will not need to touch the headset.

Step 1How the pieces fit

Before the first button, it helps to know where the words on your visor come from and where your work goes.

Good to know

ThreadX only reads. Running a procedure, jumping between steps, or finishing early changes nothing for anyone else. The one exception is RECORD PROCEDURE, which sends a video to your supervisors — see Appendix B.

Step 2Talking to the headset

Everything below works the same way in every screen of this guide, so it is worth two minutes now. Nothing here is specific to ThreadX — it is how the Navigator itself works.

Say what you see

The headset listens almost all the time. To press a button, say its label out loud, exactly as written. If the button says START, say "start". If it says REMOTE ASSIST, say "remote assist". You do not need a wake word, you do not need to lean in, and you do not need to touch anything.

Say the number instead

Where a screen offers a list, the headset paints a small grey number beside each item. Saying that number does the same thing as saying the label. On the mode screen you can say "maintenance" or "select 2" and land in the same place.

If you forget a command

Say SHOW HELP. The headset lists every command available on the screen you are looking at right now. It works everywhere, including inside ThreadX, and it is the fastest way out of being stuck. Say HIDE HELP to dismiss it.

Going backwards

Almost every screen in ThreadX accepts GO BACK. From inside a running procedure, GO HOME returns you to the mode list in one jump.

While it is reading to you

This is the one time the headset is not listening for everything. While ThreadX reads a step aloud, only two commands are live: STOP READING, which cuts the narration short, and GO BACK. Anything else you say during the reading is ignored — say it again once the voice stops.

Say STOP READING when you have heard enough. The full command list returns about a second later and you can carry on with NEXT, IMAGE or anything else on the bar.

Why it goes quiet

The headset hears its own voice through its own microphones. Without this, the narration saying "Step four" would jump you to step 4 on its own. Narrowing the commands during the reading is what stops the app talking itself into the wrong step.

In a loud plant

The Navigator's microphones are built for noise, but they are not magic. If a command is missed, say it again in the same flat, normal voice — shouting distorts the audio and makes recognition worse, not better. If several commands in a row are missed, check the boom microphone is swung down near the corner of your mouth.

Step 3Signing in

ThreadX opens on a PIN pad. One shared PIN unlocks the app for everyone on your site — ask your supervisor for it. You will not enter a username or a password.

  1. The keypad. Say the digits one at a time — "one", "two", "three", "four". Say the digit, not "press one".
  2. The four dots. They fill as you speak so you can see how far you have got. Your PIN is never shown. Say BACKSPACE to remove the last digit or CLEAR to start again.
  3. SIGN IN. The fourth digit submits automatically, so most of the time you will never say this. Use it if you cleared and retyped.

A wrong PIN clears all four dots and the prompt changes to INCORRECT PIN. There is no lockout — try again.

You're done when

The screen changes to SELECT MODE.

Step 4Choosing a mode

Five tiles. Four of them are the way into the procedure library; the fifth is the camera.

  • TRAINING, MAINTENANCE, TROUBLESHOOTING and GUIDES all open the same procedure library. The one you pick sets the heading and the tag you will see on the next screen, so your work is filed under the right heading. Pick the one that matches why you are there.
  • RECORD PROCEDURE opens the camera instead, to film a job for your supervisors. See Appendix B.
  • SIGN OUT, top right, returns to the PIN pad.
Good to know

Because all four procedure modes share one library, you will see the same list of procedures whichever you choose. If a job is not in MAINTENANCE, it is not in GUIDES either.

Step 5Picking a procedure

The library, with the heading set by the mode you chose. Choosing takes two words: the item number, then OPEN.

  1. The mode tag. Confirms which mode you came in through. It follows you all the way to the last step.
  2. A filled tick means this procedure is already saved on the headset. It will keep working if you walk out of Wi-Fi range.
  3. A download arrow means it is not saved yet. Say DOWNLOAD ITEM 4 to save it before you go out to the machine.
  4. The hint line repeats the commands this screen accepts, so you never have to remember them.

Say the number of the row you want — the highlight moves to it. Then say OPEN. Two words, because a mis-heard number in a noisy plant should not launch the wrong job.

Before you leave Wi-Fi

A procedure with a download arrow has not been saved. Out at the machine with no signal it will not open, and its pictures will not load. Download it while you are still in coverage.

To give a saved copy back, say REMOVE ITEM 2. ThreadX asks you out loud to confirm, and nothing is deleted until you say CONFIRM REMOVE. Say CANCEL, or simply say nothing for fifteen seconds, and the copy is kept. Only the offline copy goes — the procedure itself stays in the library and can be downloaded again.

Step 6Reading the brief

A summary page before anything starts, so you can check you picked the right job and know how long it will take.

  1. Duration, version and steps. Duration is a rough estimate written by whoever published the procedure, not a target. The step count is exact.
  2. CANCEL goes back to the list without starting anything.
  3. START begins the procedure at step 1.

Step 7Working through the steps

This is where you will spend the job. One step on screen at a time, read aloud to you, with the picture beside it. Your hands stay on the machine.

  1. Where you are. "Step 1 of 10", then the procedure name.
  2. The progress bar. Fills left to right as you work.
  3. The picture for this step.
  4. The instruction, which is also read aloud to you when the step opens.
  5. REMOTE ASSIST calls a live expert. See Step 9.
  6. AI ASSIST answers a spoken question about the step. Also Step 9.

The button bar

Seven buttons run along the bottom of every step. Say any of their labels.

  • PREVIOUS — back one step. Greyed out on step 1.
  • REPEAT — reads the current instruction aloud again. Use this instead of taking your hands off the machine. Say STOP READING to cut a reading short.
  • SPLIT — picture and text side by side. This is what you start in.
  • TEXT — the instruction alone, in large type, filling the visor.
  • IMAGE — the picture alone, with zoom and pan.
  • VIDEO — the step's video, if one was published with it.
  • NEXT — forward one step. On the last step it finishes the procedure.

You can also jump: say GO TO STEP 9 to land straight on step 9. Useful when you already know the machine and only need the awkward part.

  1. PREVIOUS is no longer greyed out, because there is somewhere to go back to.
  2. The progress bar jumped with you. It tracks the step you are on, not how many you have read.

Looking closer at the picture

Say IMAGE to fill the visor with the photograph. A column of buttons appears down the left.

Text only

Say TEXT when the picture is not helping and you want the words as large as the visor will draw them.

When there is no video

VIDEO is always on the bar, but not every step has a video attached. If this one does not, you get a plain notice rather than an error.

Turning the narration off

If the spoken instructions get in your way, say SETTINGS and turn Audio off. See Step 10 — and note it switches back on the next time ThreadX is restarted.

Step 8Finishing

Saying NEXT on the last step ends the procedure.

Say GO HOME to return to the mode list and start the next job.

You're done when

You see Procedure Complete. Nothing else is required of you — ThreadX does not ask you to sign off, and it does not record that you finished.

Nothing is kept

ThreadX does not remember which procedures you have run or where you got to. If you leave a job half done and come back later, you start at step 1 — so note the step number before you walk away, and use GO TO STEP to return to it.

Step 9When you get stuck

Two ways to get help without taking the headset off, both reachable from the header of any step.

AI Assist

Say AI ASSIST to open a spoken conversation about the step you are on. Ask a question out loud — why a part will not seat, what a term means, what to check next — and it answers aloud. Say CLOSE or GO BACK to return to your step exactly where you left it.

Remote Assist

Say REMOTE ASSIST to place a live video call to a support room, so an expert sees what you see. While the call is up:

  • MUTE and UNMUTE control your microphone.
  • CAMERA ON and CAMERA OFF control what they can see.
  • END CALL hangs up and returns you to your step.
Both need a working connection

AI Assist and Remote Assist are the two parts of ThreadX that will not work offline, even for a downloaded procedure. If either fails to connect, say RETRY; if it keeps failing, the fault is the network or the service behind it, not your headset. Carry on with the written steps and report it.

Not pictured

Neither of these screens is photographed in this guide. See Appendix D for why, and what it would take to add them.

Step 10Settings

Three things, reachable by saying SETTINGS from nearly any screen.

  1. About. Say ABOUT for the version number. Worth reading out when you report a problem.
  2. Language. Say ENGLISH or SPANISH. This changes the app's own wording and the voice that reads your steps. It does not translate the procedures themselves — those appear in whatever language they were written in.
  3. Audio. Say AUDIO ON or AUDIO OFF to control whether step instructions are read aloud.
These two do not stick

Language and Audio last only until ThreadX is closed. Next time the app starts it is back to English with audio on. If you work in Spanish, expect to set it each shift.

BAppendix B — Recording a procedure

The fifth tile films what you are doing and sends it to your supervisors, so a job nobody has written up yet can be turned into a procedure later. You are the camera.

Say START RECORDING to begin and STOP RECORDING to end. You are then asked what to do with the clip:

  • SEND VIDEO uploads it to your supervisors.
  • DISCARD deletes it from the headset. It is not sent, and it is not recoverable.
The upload survives you

Once you say SEND VIDEO the upload continues in the background. You can leave the screen, close ThreadX, or let the headset sleep, and it will finish on its own — including after a drop in signal.

Videos still waiting to be sent

A video you sent with no signal waits on the headset until there is one. Say PENDING UPLOADS on the recorder screen to see the queue: every clip that has not landed yet, with its length, its size, and whether it is uploading right now.

To clear one out, say DELETE ITEM 2. ThreadX asks you out loud to confirm and deletes nothing until you say CONFIRM DELETE; CANCEL keeps it. Say GO BACK to return to the recorder.

The queue empties itself

You do not have to visit this screen. A queued video uploads on its own as soon as the headset has a network, and disappears from the list once it has landed. The screen is there for when you want to know whether last week's clip actually went.

DISCARD cannot be undone

A discarded recording is gone from the headset immediately and was never sent anywhere. If you are not certain, send it — an unwanted video can be deleted by your supervisors later, but a discarded one cannot be recovered by anyone. The same is true of CONFIRM DELETE on a queued video: it never reached your supervisors, so there is no copy anywhere else.

CAppendix C — When something goes wrong

What you seeWhat it meansWhat to do
Commands are ignored The headset is not hearing you, or the screen has changed under you. Say SHOW HELP. If the panel does not appear, the headset itself is not listening — check the boom microphone position and that the headset is awake.
Commands are ignored while a step is being read aloud Expected. Only STOP READING and GO BACK are live during a reading. Say STOP READING, wait for the voice to stop, then say what you wanted.
A different screen opens A number or label was misheard. Say GO BACK and try again, using the label rather than the number.
INCORRECT PIN The four digits did not match. Say the digits again slowly. There is no lockout. If it never works, the site PIN has been changed — ask your supervisor.
VIDEO UNAVAILABLE No video was published for this step. Nothing. Say SPLIT and carry on.
Pictures do not load No signal, and this procedure was not downloaded. Return to coverage, then say DOWNLOAD ITEM and the number before going back out.
An error screen with RETRY ThreadX could not reach the network. Say RETRY. If it persists, OPEN SETTINGS to check Wi-Fi, or GO HOME and report it.
The app restarted and lost your language Expected. Language and audio are not saved. Say SETTINGS and set them again.

DAppendix D — What is not pictured

Screens this guide describes in words but does not show. None of them were faked or quietly dropped. Each line says exactly what it would take to close the gap.

#Missing shotHow to capture it
1The SHOW HELP overlay, Step 2 Cannot be triggered from a connected computer — it responds only to real speech. Wear the headset, say "show help" on the mode screen and again inside a step, and capture both. Roughly one minute.
2AI ASSIST, Step 9 Open it from any step and ask one question. Each session costs credit against the AI service, which is why it was not run for this guide.
3REMOTE ASSIST, Step 9 Needs a second person to answer the call before there is anything to photograph. Arrange ten minutes with a colleague, and crop or blur their face before publishing.

7The PENDING UPLOADS queue, Appendix B The queue is empty unless a recording is waiting, and filling it means recording and sending a real clip — the same constraint as the row above. Record one short clip with Wi-Fi off, then capture the list and the CONFIRM DELETE prompt.
8The offline and error screens, Appendix C Turn Wi-Fi off on the headset and open a procedure that has not been downloaded.
9Every screen in Spanish Say SPANISH in settings and repeat the capture. This guide is English only by decision, not by accident.
